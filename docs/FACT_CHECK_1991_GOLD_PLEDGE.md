# Fact-check notes: 1991 RBI gold pledge

This production uses the July 1991 RBI gold-pledge transaction, not the separate May 1991 SBI/UBS transaction involving 20 tonnes of confiscated government gold.

## Claims used in the Short

- The RBI dispatched 46.91 tonnes of gold in four air consignments beginning 4 July 1991.
- The loan raised against the pledge totalled US$405 million.
- The loan was repaid by November 1991.
- The pledged gold was not immediately brought back to India; it remained abroad.
- C. Rangarajan's first-person account says that, for one large consignment, the bullion van stopped because of a suspected tyre burst in one of the cars in the convoy. The script therefore does not state that the bullion van itself had a flat tyre.
- The crisis left India with foreign exchange reserves equivalent to roughly three weeks of imports. Avoid turning this into a precise "15 days until bankruptcy" countdown.

## Editorial cautions

- Do not call the 46.91-tonne July RBI pledge the same transaction as the May 1991 20-tonne SBI/UBS gold lease.
- Do not claim the gold was physically returned to India in November 1991. Repayment of the loan and physical repatriation are different events.
- Do not claim the gold pledge alone caused India's later economic growth. It helped address an immediate external-payments crisis; the 1991 reforms were a wider policy response.
- Do not use a countdown that says India had exactly 15 days before bankruptcy. "Around three weeks of import cover" is better supported.
- The first render uses stylised motion-graphics backgrounds, not historical photographs or generated scene images. Label generated recreations as illustrative if used.
